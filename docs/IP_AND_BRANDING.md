# Project Tarmin — IP & Branding

I'm not a lawyer and this isn't legal advice — treat this as a factual framing of the decision, not a clearance. Given a free public release is intended, get an actual legal opinion (or at minimum, research current fan-game precedent for this specific franchise) before release, not after.

## Stance

Distinct final name, with inspiration from *Treasure of Tarmin* (Intellivision, 1981, Mattel) acknowledged openly rather than hidden. This is a middle position between "fan project trading directly on the original name" and "silently derivative with no attribution" — it's generally the more defensible posture of the three, but "more defensible" is not "cleared."

## Immediate Naming Conflict

The working title **"Project Tarmin"** directly contains the original trademark's distinctive term. That's fine as an internal/development codename, but is inconsistent with the "distinct name" stance chosen above once public release is on the table — a public-facing name still containing "Tarmin" undercuts the distinctness this document commits to. This needs a real rename before public release; treat the current name as a placeholder, not a candidate for the release name.

## Facts Worth Knowing (Not Legal Conclusions)

- **Copyright** covers specific creative expression: art assets, exact text, code, and specific audio. Reimplementing gameplay *mechanics* (procedural mazes, use-to-identify items, real-time projectile combat) is generally understood differently than copying protected expression — but this project's stated goal is deliberately reproducing the original's algorithm and rule *shape*, which is a more aggressive position than "inspired by," and worth having reviewed specifically.
- **Trademark** covers names, logos, and branding used to identify a product in commerce. "Treasure of Tarmin," "Tarmin," and "Minotaur" (the game's later rename) are the terms to keep the public-facing name clear of.
- **Fan games and public/free release status:** free-to-play does not automatically mean safe from an IP claim — rights holders vary widely in whether they tolerate, ignore, or act against fan recreations, and that tolerance can change without notice.
- Mattel (the original publisher/IP holder) is the relevant party to research current stated policy or precedent for, if any exists.
